# aLex user guide

How to use aLex, the BookNLP analyser. Installing and starting it are in the [README](../README.md); this guide starts from the page in your browser.

aLex only reads your BookNLP files and never changes them. Its own settings, book collections, saved groups of entities, links and tags are kept in `alex-data/library.json`, fitted topic models in `alex-data/topics`, your corrections to addressees, conversations, narrators and chapters in `alex-data/annotations.json`, reference files for keywords in `alex-data/references`, and an emotion lexicon, if you load one, in `alex-data/lexicons`. `alex-data` is made in the folder you start aLex from (see the README for choosing another place).

The first time a book is opened it takes a few seconds to index; after that it loads from `alex-data/cache`. When aLex starts it reads all your books in the background, so a library of sixty books is ready within a few seconds (about five the first time, one or two afterwards).

## Where books come from

Each workspace has a **books folder** of its own (`alex-data/books` for the Default workspace). The simplest way in is to put your BookNLP output there: while the library is empty, the Library page shows where it is and an **Open books folder** button that opens it in Finder (or your file browser); it is also under **Settings → Folders**, beside the workspace's data folder. aLex finds new books when you come back to its window. To read books where they already lie, add their folders under **Settings → Folders** (in the **[...]** menu), or start aLex with `alex --books FOLDER`; each has an **Open** button too. Any of these folders can hold BookNLP's output files directly (`houn.tokens`, `houn.entities`, …), or hold exports in dated subfolders as the companion editor writes them, in which case the newest export of each book is used. The **Library** page (the first button in the top bar) shows a card for each book; on it you can:

- tick the books to analyse (the same choice as the *Books* menu; a card with an outline is in use);
- choose an earlier export of a book (*Export used*);
- give each book a title, author, year, series and tags (*Edit details* on its card; saved as you leave each field; clicking a tag chip searches for that tag);
- **choose the book's narrator** from the button on its card: it opens a list of the book's people with a search box, and *Unnamed narrator* at the top;
- **organise the library in collections**: make collections in the list on the left (a collection can sit inside another, like a folder, and a book can be in several at once), add books to them with the ＋ on a book's card, and click a collection to list its books and those of the collections inside it. Deleting a collection never deletes books;
- **export** the selected books as one `.zip` (*Export selected books (.zip)*): their BookNLP files (the export in use) and your work on them — links between entities, names, tags, notes, groups, collections, plural groups, narrator links, corrections, settings — plus your topic models for those books and your keyword reference files;
- **search** by title, author, series, tag, year or id, and group the cards by series or author; *Analyse the books shown* sets the book selection to whatever is listed, and *Add the books shown to…* files them all in a collection;
- open a book's own **Overview** (click its title): its facts (words, quotes, author, year…) and, pooling every entity counted in it as one, the same figures as an entity's page — "how does this book look" — minus Speech and Topics (which need one particular entity, not a pool) and Appears with (the pool already contains almost everyone who could appear with it).

When you export again from the companion editor, aLex picks up the new export when you return to its window.

If an export's folder also holds the original `.txt` (the companion editor copies it there), an **Original text** button appears beside that book's export, to download it; the analyser also uses it, when present, to get the exact spacing between words right in the text view (otherwise it guesses at a single space).

The **?** at the top right opens the help of the page you are on in a side panel: what the page shows and each of its options, then how aLex works everywhere (it closes with × or Escape). A small round **?** in a section's heading (on an entity's page: what they do, Book by book, Speech, What's distinctive; in Dialogue: how speech is introduced, speaking style, distinctive vocabulary, quotes; in Corpus: beside the Search button and in the search settings; beside the chapters-or-slices setting, Stylometry's settings, Sentiment, the Sound settings line and Pronunciation; Topics' Documents, Words and Model settings; Over the book's grids and curve; Described as; Compare's words; Network's filters and measures) opens it at that section. On a page with tabs, the top bar's **?** opens at the tab you are on. The **book button** (the small book icon at the top right, beside the **[...]** menu) chooses which books to analyse. Point at it to see what you are viewing and how many books are in use; click it to open a panel with a search box and filters by collection, series, author, tag or year; only its list of books scrolls (the search and buttons stay in view, and the page behind doesn't move), it stays open while you tick books and opens or closes without touching the menu, and closes on **Use these books**, **Cancel**, a click elsewhere or Escape.

What you are viewing is named in the book button's tooltip on every page: a single book's title, a whole collection's or series' name, "Various for…" for several books from the same one, or "Various" (and how many of the books are in use). The button is tinted when only some of your books are selected. Clicking a book's name in a table or chart that lists several (such as "Book by book" on an entity's page) narrows the selection to just that book; a button then appears beside the book button saying where it returns to (**← Back to all books**, **← Back to Casebook**, **← Back to 3 books**); point at it for a reminder of why it is there.

## Workspaces

A **workspace** is a separate library: its own folders of books, links between entities, groups, collections, corrections, topic models, reference files and settings. Nothing is shared or merged between workspaces, so you can keep one corpus apart from another. The workspace in use is shown on the **Workspace** row of the **[...]** menu at the top right; its submenu lists them all (**Manage workspaces…** leads to the Workspaces section of the Library page, where they are made and changed), and choosing another one opens it and reloads the page. What you saw before (book selection, search settings…) is remembered separately for each workspace. The analyser starts in the workspace you used last.

The library you start with is the **Default** workspace. Under **Library → Workspaces** you can:

- **make a workspace** (a name, and optionally the folder its books are in), then *Switch to* it; put books in its books folder, or add more book folders to it under *Settings → Folders*;
- **open a workspace's folder** in Finder (or your file browser) with *Open folder*: its books folder is inside;
- **import** one from a zip made with *Export selected books (.zip)*: the books are copied into the new workspace together with the links, groups, collections, corrections, topic models and reference files in the zip, so the zip works on any computer (and importing never touches another workspace). Whatever in the zip aLex can't read (from another version, or edited by hand) is left out and listed on the Library page;
- **rename** a workspace, or **remove it from the list** (its files stay where they are);
- **delete** a workspace the analyser made (an imported one, or one made here): this removes its folder with everything in it, including the books in its books folder, and can't be undone, so you must type its name first. The Default workspace and the one in use can't be removed or deleted, and books in folders a workspace merely points at are never touched.

The list of workspaces is kept in `alex-data/workspaces.json`, and the workspaces made or imported here in `alex-data/workspaces/`. The Default workspace is `alex-data` itself.

## Minimum mentions

Under **Settings → Entities**. There's one minimum per entity type (PER, LOC, FAC, GPE, VEH, ORG, and VAR for things you code by hand in the companion editor), and 2 matches BookNLP's rule for `.book`. You can count it in each book separately, or across the selected books combined.

## Settings

**Settings** (in the **[...]** menu) is one page with a contents list on the left: **Appearance** (the theme, also in the menu), **Entities** (whether plural groups count for their members by default, the minimum mentions per type, and how to count them), **Words** (whether a tool's *Minimum frequency* is checked for a word over the selected books together, which is the default, or in each book separately; the number itself stays in each tool, and a small line beside it says which way it counts), **Sound and rhythm** (the **Window**: how many content words apart words may be and still make a run of alliteration, assonance, consonance or rhyme, 3 by default and as large as you like; where runs stop: a **sentence end**, the default, a **paragraph end**, or **nowhere**; **Alliteration by** the *stressed syllable* (the default), the word's *first sound*, or its *first letter* as spelled; and **Repetition is** the *same lemma* (the default), the *same word form*, or the *same lemma among all words*, function words included; repetitions are runs of their own, and a repeated word never counts again in a sound run; all of these apply to the Sound and rhythm tab, Statistics and Over the book alike; **Pronunciations** lists your corrections, with the same form to add one as under Sound and rhythm → Pronunciation) and **Folders** (where to find books). It applies to the workspace in use, and choices save as you make them.

**Focus** (under *Entities*). By default every page remembers what it looks at on its own. Choose **One focus for every page** and the entity, group, tag, gender or book you choose as a page's subject, or whose page you open, becomes the subject of every page: Over the book (all three views; side A when two books are aligned), Described as, Compare's first side, Speech verbs, How they speak and In-Depth Who Speaks open on it, and their chooser says *follows the focus*. The focus is named at the top right (**Focus: Holmes ×**; × clears it). Filters (Conversations, Quotes, a speech scope), *Compared with*, second sides and Statistics keep their own choices, and a page that can't take the focus (a group where only one speaker fits) shows its own. Switching back keeps each page where it was. The choice is kept in this browser, for each workspace.

## Light and dark mode

**Theme** is in the **[...]** menu at the top right: Auto (follows your system), Light or Dark. Your choice is remembered. The same menu holds **Entity Links**, **Narrators**, the **Workspace**, **Plural groups** and **Settings**; its submenus open to the left when you point at them or click them, and the menu closes when you choose something, click elsewhere or press Escape.

## What the figures are based on

Beside some figures a small line starts **Based on:** and names the settings and corrections they depend on, so you can see what was counted without looking for it. On an entity's page the line under the name gives the minimum mentions, whether plural groups count for their members, and whether you linked the entity across books; *Book by book* adds how a minimum frequency is checked, *Over the book* how the book is cut (click it to change that in place), and *Speech* the narration words that end a conversation, the words you left out of the speech verbs, and how many quotes by them or to them have addressees you set. A **tinted** word is set by you: a setting you changed from its default, or one of your corrections. Click a word to go where it is changed (the right section of Settings, the Dialogue page, Entity Links or the quotes).

## Numbers under a chart

Where a chart draws something chapter by chapter or slice by slice (Along the book on Over the book and Compare, Arcs, Sentiment, Lexicons, a topic's arc), the table of its numbers is folded away under the chart, as *Numbers per slice* or *Numbers per chapter or slice*: click it to open the table (with its CSV link). It starts folded on every visit; one you open stays open while you change the chart.

## Read with care: ≈

A small **≈** after a figure or a heading marks one to read with care, and pointing at it says why: a figure from a small sample (MATTR from fewer than 100 words, a correlation or a mean from fewer than 30 cases on the Statistics page), an estimate (whom a quote is addressed to, which BookNLP doesn't record), a topic that came back in fewer than 60% of the re-runs, or the sound figures of a book or slice with more than 10% of its pronunciations guessed. The figure is still shown, and still in the CSV; the mark only says how far to trust it.

## Long pages: side menu and collapsing

- **Side menu.** A page with three or more sections (an entity's profile, Library, Links, Narrators, the topic pages, stylometry and so on) gets a sticky menu of them, which highlights the one you're reading. It always sits at the right. **Collapse all** and **Expand all** are under it. Topic pages have their own menu, at the right too. The » button next to *On this page* folds the menu to a thin strip and lets the page use the whole window width; « brings it back. Each page remembers this on its own. The menu is hidden on narrow windows.
- **Collapsing.** Click the arrow beside any section heading, or the heading itself, to fold the section away. Sub-headings (such as the blocks under stylometry) fold too, together with what follows them. Your choices are remembered by page and heading, so they stay after a reload.

## Many books

The analyser is built to work on a whole library: it has been run on sixty books (418,000 tokens, 4,700 entities) and on 180 books (1.25 million tokens) cut from real BookNLP output. Sixty books take about 400 MB of memory while it runs, and 180 about 800 MB. Most pages answer in well under a second; a few things take a few seconds the first time (style figures, sentiment, link suggestions). The first start parses every book (about 3 seconds for sixty short ones, a minute or two for sixty novels; the terminal says how far it is); later starts read the parsed copies it keeps. To keep results readable and quick:

- a **network** shows the 300 most strongly linked entities (change **Show at most** on the Network page, up to 1,000; or raise the minimum link strength) and says how many there were;
- a **concordance** stops at 20,000 hits and shows 500 lines at a time (**Show more**); its plot shades each slice of a book by the number of hits;
- **word lists** and **n-grams** list the 5,000 most frequent;
- charts with many books name their lines by hover instead of at the line's end, the strips showing where something occurs give a book's total (not one figure per slice) beyond 30 books, and "Kinds of action, book by book" shows the eight books with most actions.

To see how your own library behaves, and which request is slow, run this from a clone of the repository (with the environment active):

```
python tests/benchmark.py --sources /path/to/your/books
```

It only reads your books; it prints each request's time and size and ends with the memory used. To try a bigger library than you have, `python tests/make_corpus.py --from <folders of BookNLP output> --out <folder> --books 60 --copies 3` cuts books into pieces and writes them several times over.

## Tests and documentation

- **How it works, and how to change it:** [DOCUMENTATION.md](DOCUMENTATION.md) explains the folder layout, the data files, how a request flows, the methods used, the web API, the browser code and how to add a feature. The code carries comments on every function.
- **Tests:** a suite of about 1,000 tests checks the analysis against a small generated corpus with known answers, every web route (also with input of the wrong kind, which must give a readable message and never a crash), damaged and unusual files, a library of sixty generated books, and (in a simulated browser) every page and the main features, also with sixty-four books and with none. To run it, from a clone of the repository:

  ```
  pip install -e ".[dev]"
  cd tests/js && npm install        # optional: adds the browser tests (needs Node)
  cd ../.. && pytest
  ```

  The tests use their own generated books and temporary folders; they never touch your data.

## Views

The top bar groups the pages into **areas**, each answering one kind of question: **Library**; **Entities** (Entities, Compare, Described as, Network); **Dialogue** (Who speaks, Speech verbs, How they speak, Conversations, Quotes, In-Depth Who Speaks); **Words** (the Corpus tools: Concordance, Word list, N-grams, Collocates, Keywords; and Topics); **Style** (Style, Stylometry, Sound and rhythm); **Over time** (Over the book, Arcs, Sentiment, Lexicons); **Statistics**; and **Read**. An area's pages are the tabs at the top of the page; clicking an area opens the page you last used in it (remembered per workspace). Links to a page from before still work. The views below are described page by page.

- **Entities.** One page per person, place, organisation and so on, its sections in the order of the questions they answer (the side menu groups them the same way):
  - *who they are*: mentions and where they fall in each book; names, descriptions and pronouns, and who names them in dialogue; who they appear with;
  - *what they do*: actions, what's done to them, possessions, modifiers, and prepositions ("went to London"), with events, and the kinds of action (supersenses) under the table;
  - *what they say*: their speech;
  - *how they change*: book by book, and a preview of Over the book (mentions slice by slice; the words and kinds of action are on the Over the book page);
  - *what sets them apart*: what's distinctive about them compared with others, and their topics.
  Click any row to see the sentences behind it. Add tags here. **Rename** an entity (or a linked person) from its own name, and give it a short
  **description** — both shown everywhere that entity is, and undone by clearing them (a "Reset" link brings back the name found in the books).
  - **Narrators** are entities too: the **Narrators** chip lists one row per narrator role ("Watson, narrating", "Narrator of *book*"). A role's profile shows how much it narrates and where, its voice (questions, I/you/we, word length, MATTR) next to all narration and next to the same character's dialogue, a row per book, and the words that are distinctive of its narration. The character's own page is unchanged.
  - **Gender** is a pool too: the **Gender** chip lists one row per BookNLP-predicted gender (he/him/his, she/her, they/them/their…, plus "No BookNLP gender prediction" for anyone without one). Its page pools every entity of that gender as one — "how do the women in these books talk, act, appear" — the same figures as an entity's page, minus Speech and Topics (which need one particular entity, not a pool) and Appears with (a pool already contains almost everyone who could appear with it).
  - **Several entities as a group.** Switch on **Select several** under the search box: every row gets a checkbox (click the row or the box). *Select shown* ticks everything that matches the type chip and the filter, *Select by tag…* adds every entity with a tag, *Clear* empties the ticks. **Analyse together** opens one profile for the ticked entities: they are listed at the top as removable tags with each entity's share of the group, and every figure below is pooled (mentions, how they're referred to, actions and other relations, speech, topics, who they appear with, what is distinctive). *Save as a group…* keeps the group under a name (open it from *Saved groups…*, change it and *Update* it, or use it on the Compare page).
- **Book by book** (on an entity's page, when it's in two or more of the selected books). Each book's figures side by side, and a table of the entity's actions, possessions, modifiers and so on per book, with a χ² test of which words change between books. There are also charts of the most changed words and kinds of action per book. Books are listed by publication year (a book with none sorts last, marked "publication date missing" — set it under Library); click a book's name to switch to just that book.
- **Over the book** (on every profile: an entity's, a group's, a book's or a gender's). A preview of how it changes slice by slice through one book: a line chart of how often it is mentioned (per 1,000 words), with a **Book** switch when it is in several selected books. Its words and kinds of action slice by slice are on the Over the book page itself (one home per feature). The book is cut as set under **Measure over** (shared with the Style and Over time pages); the line *Based on: cut into …* opens that setting here. The first line ends “See **Over the book** for more details.”: its link opens the Over the book page with this target and book chosen, where the heat grids, trends and tests are.
- **Dialogue.** Six views:
  - **Who speaks:** dialogue per book and across each book (with more than eight books, the curve is their average, every book a faint line behind it), and a table of speakers (two of the same name, each in one book, get their book: *Holmes (alpha)*) with words spoken, share of dialogue, how often they're addressed and how their quotes are introduced.
  - **Speech verbs:** verbs and adverbs that introduce quotes ("said quietly", "cried"), overall and per speaker, and which are distinctive of a speaker; both the overall table and the one-speaker-or-group panel below it can be scoped to just questions, exclamations or statements, so you can see how, say, questions are introduced, down to one speaker. If a word shows up that doesn't introduce speech (a verb BookNLP mislabels, an adverb that isn't manner), list it under *Leave out verbs and adverbs*; the list is kept per workspace.
  - **How they speak:** a speaking-style table (words per quote, questions, exclamations, I/you/we, word length, MATTR, and word-choice columns: parts of speech, adjectives per verb, dynamic verbs…, chosen above the table) and each speaker's distinctive vocabulary against other speakers, one speaker or a tag group. Both can be scoped to just questions, exclamations or statements.
  - **Conversations:** runs of quotes; click one to read it with speakers and estimated addressees.
  - **Quotes:** every quote, filtered by speaker, book, text, speech verb, attribution, how the addressee was found, or sentence type (questions, exclamations, statements).
  - **In-Depth Who Speaks:** Who speaks' three sections — Dialogue in each book (including its "Share of words in dialogue" chart), Speakers and Narrators — filtered or compared by **Entity**, **Group**, **Type**, **Tag** or **Gender**, everyone the choice matches pooled as one (the same way the Gender and tag pages elsewhere pool a bucket into one profile). **Search** mode scopes all three sections, chart included, to one choice (or shows everyone, same as Who speaks, with nothing chosen). **Compare** mode (like Corpus's Simple/Pattern toggle) puts two choices' figures side by side as rows rather than columns — a paired bar per book for Dialogue in each book, a shared chart with one coloured line per side for the dialogue-share curve, and a paired bar per figure for Speakers and Narrators, target's colour above reference's throughout — so you can see, say, how women's dialogue volume compares with men's, book by book, or one tagged group against another.
  - **Sentence type** (Quotes, How they speak's Speaking style and Distinctive vocabulary, Speech verbs' overall table and its one-speaker-or-group panel, and an entity's Speech section): a quote is a question or exclamation if it contains `?` or `!`; tick **Weigh the speech verb too** to also count one like *asked* as a question or *exclaimed*/*cried* as an exclamation when the quote itself has neither mark. Every one of these has its own Sentence type and checkbox: setting one never changes what another shows.
- **Columns.** A table of seven columns or more has a small **Columns: 8 of 17** link beside its CSV link: tick the columns to show (grouped where the table has groups, such as the sound table's stress, cadences, runs and sound profile), or choose *Show all* or *Default*. The widest tables (style, sound, speaking style, narrators, Book by book, speech per book, network measures) open on their main figures; a column of your corrections or of a warning (*Set by paragraph*, *Guessed pronunciations*) always shows. Your choice is remembered per table, and the CSV always has every column.
- **Scrolling results.** Tables, concordance lines and quote lists are at most one screen tall. Scroll with the pointer over one and it moves first; at its top or bottom the whole page moves again. Table headers stay in view inside the box.
- **Corpus.** Tools after AntConc, over the selected books. Each can be limited to narration, dialogue, or the speech of an entity, a group of several entities, a tag or a gender (the same chooser as in Dialogue). The tool tabs scroll away with the page, and search settings (below) are always visible, not tucked behind a click.
  - **Concordance (KWIC):** sortable on three levels, each with a position (the hit, one of five words left or right of it, or the book) and what to compare there: the word, its lemma, its word class (POS), its fine tag, or how often that word occurs there among the hits; an arrow reverses a level. A plot shades each slice of every book by the number of hits, with hits per book, dispersion, and who says them. Click a line to read its paragraph. A search stops at 20,000 hits; scope, near-word and context filters are applied before that limit, so they never use it up.
  - **Search in the context** (under the search box): keep only hits that have, or do not have, a second search in the words around them. It takes a simple or pattern query like the main search, how many words to look at on the left and the right, and can stay within the sentence.
  - **Word list:** words, lemmas, word + POS, **word + POS + lemma** (three columns) or POS, with frequency, range and dispersion.
  - **Word types:** the word list and the collocates can be limited to word classes (POS), entity types and fine tags, several of each (words of any chosen value, and every group that has a choice must match). In a word list rates stay per 1,000 words of the whole text.
  - **N-grams:** a range of lengths, optionally containing a word first, last or anywhere, like AntConc's clusters.
  - **Collocates:** a window you choose, ranked by MI, T-score, logDice, MI3, log-likelihood or frequency; optionally only collocates of some word types.
  - **Keywords:** against other books (any of your library, with their own scope, such as dialogue against narration) or reference files. Reference files can be word lists (such as AntConc's exports) or plain texts.
  - **Searches are simple by default:** words with `*`, `?`, `a|b` and `#` for any word.
  - **Pattern queries** search BookNLP's layers, for example `[lemma="say"] [pos="ADV"]` or `[char="Holmes"] [lemma="look"]`. Attributes: word, lemma, pos, tag, dep, ent, prop, char, ss, event, quote, speaker. Quantifiers: `? * + {n,m}`.
  - **Batch search** (Concordance and Collocates): tick **Batch** to turn the search box into a list, one term or pattern per line — `said`, `asked`, `shouted`, or a pattern per line — searched together as alternatives and merged into one result. Quicker than typing `said|asked|shouted` by hand, and, unlike that, a line can be a whole phrase or pattern. Turn on the **Matched** checkbox under the results to see, in its own column, which line found each hit.
  - **Regular expressions** can be turned on under Search settings.
  - **Wildcards**, also under Search settings, are on by default (`*`, `?`, `a|b` and `#` as above). Turn them off to search for those characters literally instead — useful for finding punctuation like `?` or `!` — in either Simple or Pattern search; it's disabled while Regular expressions is on, since regex already gives you that.
- **Style and Over time** (Style and Stylometry in the Style area; Arcs, Sentiment and Lexicons in Over time). Everything here can be measured over equal slices of each book (a number per book, or one every so many words, such as 200) or over chapters. Chapters are found from headings (Chapter, Adventure, numerals, lines in capitals, optionally short unpunctuated lines); check them under "Check the chapters found", and correct them in the text: **Edit chapters** opens the book like the text view, where you can start a chapter at any paragraph, rename a chapter or remove a chapter's start (its text joins the chapter before). Your changes are used everywhere chapters are (Style, Arcs, topic documents, the text view) and can be undone with *Back to the automatic chapters*. With more than one book selected, **Timeline** chooses how Arcs, sentiment and emotion plot them: **Book by book** (the default) gives each book its own line, so a trend never seems to cross a book's end; **Whole corpus** draws one continuous line across every selected book instead, to follow a change across or between them, and (with equal slices) shares the slice count across the books by their length rather than giving each the same number; **Books combined** (on Arcs, Sentiment and Lexicons) cuts every book into the same number of equal slices and puts them together slice by slice, as on Over the book: slice 5 of 10 is the same stretch of every book, each book weighs the same (a slice's value is the mean of the books' own values; a character not in a book leaves that book out), and **Show each book** draws every book's own line faintly behind. It needs equal slices by number, so it is greyed out with chapters or words per slice. **Style** has a table of style figures per book, chapter or slice, the parts of speech, **word choice** (adjectives, adverbs, verbs and nouns as shares of words, adjectives per verb, dynamic against static verbs, every kind of verb or noun; the same figures as on Dialogue and Statistics). **Chart** draws any style or word-choice figure, per book as bars or along the books by chapter or slice. MEASURES.md section 17 explains each.
  - **Arcs:** characters and places (a character of one book has no line in the other books, and two chosen characters of the same name are told apart by their book: *Holmes (alpha)*), events, supersenses and dialogue across each book, per 1,000 words, and topics (each topic's share of the words in every chapter or slice, from a topic model you choose).
  - **Style:** per book or per chapter or slice. Sentence and word length, MATTR, hapaxes, lexical density, dependency distance, tree depth, subordinate clauses, passives, questions, dialogue share, Flesch scores, and a parts-of-speech profile.
  - **Stylometry:** Burrows' Delta, Cosine Delta or Euclidean distance on the most frequent words, between books or between chapters or slices. The page has three parts: *Compare* (whether the texts are whole books or chapters or slices, and the *Method* row: most frequent words, culling, distance, tree, text, pronouns), *Cluster tree and map* side by side on a wide window (coloured by book, author or series, chosen above them), and *Nearest texts and distances*.
  - **Sentiment:** a VADER sentiment arc, also around chosen characters.
  - **Lexicons:** your own word lists. Load a text, .csv or .tsv file, or type or paste one into a box: either one word on each line (the list is one category, named after the lexicon), a word and a category on each line, optionally with 1 or 0 (as in the NRC Emotion Lexicon), or one column for each category with the category names in the first row. Columns may be separated by tabs, commas or semicolons, and a header row is skipped. Each lexicon is a collapsible panel that plots the categories you choose as words per 1,000 across the books, and lets you rename it, replace its words, remove it, and browse, search and edit its words and categories. The first panel is always the emotion lexicon (such as the NRC Emotion Lexicon, which is free for research but can't be included). Words match by their form, else by their lemma. Every lexicon is also offered as variables in Statistics. A lexicon you haven't opened yet starts folded, and a folded panel loads nothing until you open it.
- **Speech** (on an entity's page): their quotes, words, share of dialogue, where they speak, speech verbs and adverbs, per book, and who they talk to and are spoken to by; a small Sentence type select (with its own "Weigh the speech verb" checkbox) scopes this to just questions, exclamations or statements, independently of every Dialogue tab's filter.
- **Sound and rhythm** (needs the pronunciation dictionary: `pip install "alex-booknlp-analyser[sound]"`). The rhythm and sound of the prose, for the **Text** you choose: everywhere, narration only, dialogue only, or someone's speech (an entity, a group, a tag or a gender, as on the Corpus pages). Four sub-tabs. **Figures**: stressed syllables, clashes and lapses, how evenly stresses are spaced (nPVI), the cadences, runs of each kind and the sound profile, one row per book, or per chapter or slice (cut as set under Measure over), with a chart of the figure you choose. **Cadences**: how phrases end, the rhythm from the last-but-one stress to the pause (/ stressed, x unstressed): the four cursus forms (planus / x x / x, tardus / x x / x x, velox / x x x x / x, trispondaicus / x x x / x) and every other pattern, each as a share of all phrase ends and of sentence ends; click a pattern for its phrases (tick *Examples: sentence ends only* for those that end a sentence). **Runs**: choose alliteration, assonance, consonance, rhyme or repetition, and see what the runs share (a sound, a letter or a repeated word) with how many runs and words each has; click one for its runs, longest first, each in its sentence, with words that repeat one nearby outlined, so repetitions stand out inside the sound runs too. **Scansion**: a chapter or slice of one book word by word (its first 3,000 words), each word with its stresses above it (in red when guessed), the runs of the kind you choose coloured (one colour per shared sound or word), repetitions outlined, the cursus cadences underlined and named (tick *Every cadence* for all of them), and words outside the chosen text faded. **Pronunciation**: how many words are guessed (not in the dictionary: mostly names, places and old spellings), the guessed words most frequent first, and your corrections. To correct a word, type it and click **Look up** (it shows how it is said now and the dictionary's version, to start from), then type how it is said: its stresses (1 0 2 or /x/) or its sounds as the dictionary writes them (B AE1 S K ER0 V IH2 L), and **Save**; click a guessed word to start from its guess. A correction counts everywhere at once and is kept with the workspace. Above Figures, Runs and Scansion a bar holds the run settings (**Run window**, **Runs stop at**, **Alliteration by**, **Repetition is**): change one and the page is counted again at once; they are the workspace's, the same as under Settings → Sound and rhythm, and the same bar is beside the Read view's sound layer.
- **Topics.** Topic models over the selected books (only those, so the selection decides what is modelled). Each model is saved with its settings, so it doesn't change under you.
  - The page opens with a short line, then your **models** and the form to **fit a new model**. The form shows what most fits change (documents, which words, method, number of topics); the finer settings (minimum documents, maximum share, minimum letters, general words, extra words to remove, seed, runs, LDA's alpha and beta) are folded under **Fine-tuning**, whose line names their values and tints any you changed from the default.
  - **Documents:** chunks of about N words (ending at a sentence), groups of paragraphs, chapters or slices (from the Measure over setting), or whole books; narration and dialogue, or either alone.
  - **Words:** nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs and proper nouns, as lemmas or word forms. Names can be removed using BookNLP's mentions (proper names, all people, or all entities), which is more precise than a name list. General words (thing, way, say, go…) can be removed, plus any of your own.
  - **Methods:** NMF (the default, usually crisper on small collections) or LDA, with a fixed seed so results repeat.
  - **Number of topics:** "Compare" fits a model for a range of numbers and shows coherence, diversity and stability for each. Click a row to use it.
  - **Overview:** each topic with its most probable and most distinctive words, share of the text, share in each book, its strongest passages, coherence and stability (in how many re-runs with other seeds a similar topic came back). Name a topic by typing in its title; the words it rests on always stay visible.
  - **Topic page** (click a topic). Name it at the top, and move between topics. The list at the left jumps between its sections and follows you as you scroll.
    - **Words:** a slider trades frequent words against exclusive ones (relevance, as in LDAvis), with each word's probability, uses and how much of it belongs to this topic. Click a word to search for it in the concordance.
    - **Where it occurs:** a strip per book shaded by the topic's share of each document (click to open the text), a table of which books use it more or less than the others (Mann–Whitney U on documents), and an arc across chapters or slices, following the Measure over setting.
    - **Passages:** the documents where the topic is strongest, with its 30 most probable words highlighted. Filter by book.
    - **Entities:** characters, places and other entities mentioned more (or less) in text where the topic is strong, by type. The score ranks them; it isn't a significance test.
    - **Speech:** how much of the topic's text is dialogue, where its top words fall, and which speakers and narrators carry it.
    - **Book details:** the topic's share by series, author, year or tag.
  - **Compare topics** (from the overview or the left of a topic page).
    - **Map of topics:** topics are similar when they share words (cosine of their word weights) or appear in the same chunks (correlation of their shares). You get a similarity network (circles sized by share, lines above a similarity you set; click a circle for the topic or a line for the pair), a cluster tree, a similarity matrix in tree order, and a table of pairs.
    - **Side by side:** two topics with words only in the first, in both, and only in the second; their arcs overlaid; their share in each book; and the entities and speakers that lean towards one rather than the other.
    - **Grids:** topic × book (or series, author, year, tag), topic × character (choose entity types) and topic × speaker. Cells show each item's share of mentions or words in each topic's text, or that against the topic's average. Every grid downloads as CSV.
  - **In the text view.** Switch on **Topics** under Show and choose a model that includes the book. A band above each passage names its leading topics (click it to see every topic and its share), a bar marks the passage's main topic, and each topic's most probable words are underlined in that topic's colour. Or choose one topic to shade passages by how much of it they hold.
  - **On an entity's page.** A Topics section shows which topics the entity's mentions, or its speech, fall in: the share of them in each topic's text, that against the topic's average, and a ranking score.
  - **Small collections give unstable topics.** Check the stability figures before reading much into a topic.
- **Read.** The text of any selected book as one scroll: chapters (or slices, following the Measure over setting) load as you scroll, each with a heading, and every paragraph has its number in the margin. The menus at the top (book, what to show, the type legend) stay frozen above a position bar that shows where you are (chapter, paragraph) and jumps between chapters, so they never slide under the text. It can show entities (underlined in their type's colour; click a type in the legend to hide or show it), quotes with speaker → addressees, each paragraph's narrator, events, supersenses, sentence numbers and topics. "Open in the text" links from sentences, quotes, concordance lines and conversations jump to the spot. Click a name for its profile, a quote to correct it, or a narrator label to change a paragraph's narrator. With the dictionary installed, the layer **Sound and rhythm** puts each word's stresses above it (/ stressed, x unstressed; red when guessed), colours the runs of the kind you choose (**Colour the runs of** alliteration, assonance, consonance, rhyme or repetition; one colour per shared sound or word; hover a word to see it), outlines repetitions, and underlines and names the last word of each cursus cadence. Clicking a pronoun or description ("he", "the detective") shows what it's **attributed to** ("Attributed to Holmes"), with a link to that entity's own profile — not just the word itself.
- **Your corrections.** In the text view, and under "Correct" on any quote in the Dialogue lists, you can:
  - set a quote's addressees (one or several, or "everyone present");
  - add listeners to a conversation, or remove participants;
  - start a new conversation at a quote, or join a conversation to the previous one.
  
  Corrections replace the estimates everywhere (tables, network, profiles) and are marked as yours. Speakers and mentions are still corrected in the companion editor.
- **Narrators.** Choose each book's narrator (a character, or an unnamed narrator) under Library or in the text view, and assign paragraphs told by someone else — one at a time, several in a row, or "Apply to this whole chapter" to give a whole chapter to someone else at once. Narration (text outside quotes) is that narrator's voice. A character who narrates appears twice, "narrating" and in dialogue, so you can compare their narration with their direct speech (Dialogue → Who speaks → Narrators, and How they speak).
- **Linking narrators.** On a narrator's own page, link it with another narrator role to treat them as one voice — Watson narrating one book and Watson narrating another, or two unnamed narrators, or a mix. This only pools their narration; everything else about them (mentions, relations, dialogue) stays separate. Unlink one at a time or all at once.
- **Narrators** (its own page, next to Entity Links). Suggestions from the "I" in each book's narration (outside quotes):
  - **The book's narrator:** the character behind most first-person narration, with each candidate's share.
  - **"Who is this?":** when BookNLP left the narrator's "I" as a group without a name, link it to a character here. This uses the same links as everywhere else.
  - **Paragraphs told by someone else:** runs of paragraphs whose "I" belongs to another character, shown with sample sentences (the "I"s marked), how much evidence there is, and Accept, Not right, or Open in the text. Accepted runs become paragraph exceptions, which you can undo. Rejections are remembered.
  - **Third-person books** have no "I" in the narration, so they get no suggestions; choose a narrator by hand if you want one.
  - Suggestions are only as good as BookNLP's grouping of "I" and its quote boundaries, so merge a split narrator and fix missing quote marks in the companion editor first.
- **Over the book.** How a target changes from the start of a book to its end: an entity, a group, a tag or a gender, in one book at a time (a **Book** switch appears when it is in several of the selected books). The book you choose is remembered across the whole feature, the page, the profile panel, Compare and Two books aligned, and stays when you change the target: an entity that isn't in it is shown in its own first book, and the choice comes back with the next entity that is. The book is cut as on the Style and Arcs pages (**Measure over** equal slices, a number per book or one every so many words, or chapters; the timeline choice doesn't apply here). **Compare with…** adds a second target; as on the Compare page, the second leaves out everything the first includes. The page has three sections: *Along the book* first (the curve, described below), then *Words over the book*, then *Slice by slice*. *Words over the book* shows the words of one list (actions, done to them, possessions, modifiers, prepositions): a line chart of the first eight in the order you chose, then each word's share of that slice's list. In the **Heat grid** every slice is a small cell, darker the bigger the word's share compared with its own highest one; **Numbers** gives the count and share in each slice. *Trend z* says whether the share steadily rises (positive, ↑) or falls through the book, with its p; χ² and Cramér's V say whether it differs between slices at all. Order the words by frequency, rising, falling or most changed. Click a cell for the sentences of that slice, a row for those of the whole book. Changing the list keeps your place on the page. Its last tab, *Kinds of action*, does the same for the kinds of action (WordNet supersenses) of the verbs it is the subject of, with the same View, Order and test settings in one row above. *Along the book* draws one figure as a line for each target, with its numbers folded under the chart (*Numbers per slice*). Choose a category, like the types of the entity selector, and then an item from its list: **Figures** (mentions per 1,000 words, names, descriptions or pronouns, a list per 100 mentions), **Words** (one word's share of a list of your choice, Actions, Done to them, Possessions…, set beside the words and separate from the one chosen under Words over the book; a line beside it says which list the words are from, whose they are and in what order: those of the chosen list that the target has, most frequent first, and with two targets the first's, then the second's that the first doesn't have, the first 100) or **Actions** (one kind of action's share); each category keeps the item you last chose in it. *Slice by slice* is the overview: words, mentions, mentions per 1,000 words, how it is referred to, and each list per 100 mentions. Tick **Sound of their speech** (beside the curve; it needs the pronunciation dictionary) to measure what the target says in each slice too: the curve gets a **Sound of speech** category (stress, cadences, alliteration… of its quotes) and the overview a table of those figures with the words spoken per slice. It works on all three sub-tabs, and the choice is remembered. With two targets each gets its own words, kinds of action and overview. Every table has its own **CSV** link. With few occurrences and many slices the tests are only a guide: raise the minimum frequency.
  - **Two books aligned** (the second sub-tab of Over the book). A target in one book against the same target, or another, in another book, lined up by *relative position*: both books are cut into the same number of equal slices (**Slices per book**, 2 to 200, its own setting; chapters and slices of a fixed number of words can't be lined up), so slice 5 of 20 is the same stretch of each, whatever the books' lengths. **Side A** takes a target; **Side B** is the same target unless you untick *Same target as A* and choose another; each side's **book** is chosen below (the books its target is in; side B starts in a different one). Nothing is left out of side B. The page has the words and kinds of action of each side (named by target and book, with the heat grids, trends and tests of the first sub-tab, and the sentences of a cell from its own book), then *Along the book*: one figure, a line per side, and under the chart **how alike the two curves are**, Pearson's r and Spearman's ρ with p over the slices that have a value on both sides. Neighbouring slices aren't independent, so read r and ρ as the size of the likeness and p as a rough guide.
  - **Books combined** (the third sub-tab of Over the book). What holds across *all* the selected books: choose a target (an entity linked across books, a group, a tag, a gender, or every person), and every selected book that has it is cut into the same number of equal slices (**Slices per book**, 2 to 200, its own setting), so slice 5 of 20 is the same stretch of each. The books are put together slice by slice and **every book weighs the same**, whatever its length: a share in a slice is the mean of the books' own shares there, while counts are added up. The page lists the books used (and those left out because the target is not in them), then the words and kinds of action with the heat grids of the first sub-tab, and the tests made *in each book* and then combined: the **trend** (z and p) puts the books' own steady rises and falls together, so books that agree make a surer trend and books that disagree cancel out; **Books**, **Rising** and **Falling** say how many books it is made from and how many go each way; **χ²** is each book's χ² added up, which tells you that a share varies within the books, not that the books agree. The minimum frequency counts the word over all the books together, and **Minimum range for the test** (next to it, 1 by default; *range* is the number of books, as on the Corpus pages) is the fewest books a word must occur in to be tested, so that a trend does not rest on one or two books; words below either are listed without tests. A cell opens the sentences of that slice in every book (the first 150, book by book), a row those of the whole books. *Along the books* draws one figure; tick **Show each book** to see every book's own line faintly behind the combined one (hover a faint line for its book). Cramér's V and the residuals are left out here.
- **Described as.** What an entity, a group, a tag or a gender is described and called as, **and by whom**: "what is the moor in *The Hound of the Baskervilles* described as by Watson, by the women, by the men?" Open it from the **Described by whom →** link on an entity's, group's, book's or gender's page, or choose what to look at (under **Group**, *or add every entity named…* gathers every entity whose name contains your words, such as `moor, mire`, optionally of one type: BookNLP often splits one place into dozens of entities). Then choose what to count: **Modifiers** (desolate, vast…), what it is **called** (names, descriptions such as "the great Mire", pronouns), its **actions** and what is **done to it**, **possessions** and **prepositions and settings**. Each finding is credited to who says it: the speaker of the quote it is in, or, outside quotes, the narrator of the paragraph (set narrators on the Narrators page; a narrator who is a character counts as that character, so Watson is his speech and his narration together). The table has a row per word and a column per describer. **Split by** chooses the columns: each describer, the describer's gender (BookNLP's prediction), the describer's tag, the book, narration against speech, or **Columns you pick**: your own columns, each an entity, group, tag or gender chosen with the usual picker (for example Watson | Holmes | all women), up to twelve; the same entity can be in several columns, and what none of your columns say is only in the All column; **Voice** keeps only narration or only speech; **Columns** is how many columns to show (the rest are gathered in "All others"). Beside each count you can show the **share** of that column's findings, the rate **per 1,000 words** that column's describers say, and the **keyness** (G²: + means more typical of that column than of the others) and its **p-value**; blue cells are markedly typical of their column and red ones markedly untypical. **Click any count** to read the sentences behind it (the word is marked, and each sentence says who said it: "Watson (narration)", "Holmes (speech)"); there are exactly as many as the count.
- **Statistics.** Correlations and descriptive statistics of anything aLex can count. First choose the **cases**, the rows of the data: **text pieces** (equal slices, by default: a number per book, or one every so many words, such as 200; sentences, chapters or whole books, optionally only their narration, dialogue or someone's speech) or **participants** (characters taken as people in a study: every person, or the entities, group, tag or gender you choose), with their **sample**: what they say, what they say and narrate, the sentences that mention them, or what is said about them (their Described-as findings). The page is three steps: **1 Cases**, **2 Variables** and **3 Run** (a saved set is opened or saved on the line above them). Then add **variables** with the **+ Add a variable…** menu: **Words** (a word, lemma, phrase or pattern, written as on the Corpus page), **Mentions** (of an entity, group, tag or gender; names, descriptions or pronouns), **Speech** (of an entity, group, tag or gender: the words they speak or their quotes, optionally only their questions, exclamations or statements; quotes also as a % of quotes), **Attribute** (for participants: whether they are one of a gender, tag or group, as 0/1, their mentions, words spoken, quotes, books; for pieces: their number, position and length) and, under *Layer*, (supersenses, relations such as "actions of Holmes: say", the **speech sentence type** (how many quotes are questions, exclamations or statements, with the same "weigh the speech verb" choice as on the Dialogue tabs, also as a % of quotes), sentiment, a category of a lexicon such as your emotion lexicon, style figures, **sound and rhythm** figures (stress, cadences, sound runs, the sound profile, or a single sound; they need the pronunciation dictionary and use the window and boundary under Settings), and **topics** of a topic model: a topic's share of each case's words; a model fitted on only some of the selected books leaves the other books' cases without a value, and the page warns you). Each counted variable is **per 1,000 words** of the sample, a **count**, or **present** (0/1). Several at once, from the **Add several…** menu: **Paste a list**, the **most frequent words** (optionally only some word classes, a **sentence type** so that only words inside questions, exclamations or statements are considered, **Speech verbs only** for the verbs that introduce quotes as in Dialogue → Speech verbs, and **Minimum range**: only words in at least that many of the selected books; the variables made still count the word everywhere in the sample), the **most mentioned entities**, the **most talkative speakers** (those who speak the most words, as speech variables), **every gender**, **every tag**, **every saved group**, **every style figure**, **every sound figure** (the first 30), **every category of a lexicon**, **every topic of a model**. **Clear** empties a list (after asking). ## How the numbers are made

Each table has a "How…" note underneath explaining it. In brief:

- **Relations** use BookNLP's rules on the dependency parse, with three additions:
  - conjoined mentions share a role ("Holmes and Watson went");
  - a subject counts for coordinated verbs ("rose and lit his pipe");
  - prepositional attachments are recorded for every type.
  Counts can therefore be slightly higher than in `.book`.
- **Plural groups** (e.g. "Holmes and Watson" and the *they* BookNLP linked to it) are entities of their own. Declare one on its page (in
  its framed "Plural group" box: search for a member and click it; × removes one), from the entity list (tick the entities, then "Make plural group of the others…"), or
  accept a suggestion on the Entities page (entities named after two or more others). On a linked entity it applies in every book (one
  made before you linked the entity carries over). A page only shows the members counted in the selected books (and says how many are below the
  minimum); adding or removing one there keeps the others.
  Whether their mentions, what they do, who they appear with, their quotes and the quotes addressed to them also count for each member
  is up to you: **Plural groups** in the **[...] menu** (also under **Entities** on the Settings page) switches it on or off for every page at once (off, to begin with; each workspace has its own). A member named inside a plural mention ("Holmes and Watson walked")
  counts once, through its own mention, and a plural group is never counted as appearing with its own members.
- **Change between books** uses Pearson's χ² for each word against all other words, by book, with adjusted standardised residuals to show which books differ.
- **Addressees** aren't in BookNLP's output, so they're estimated, and each estimate records how:
  1. a person named in the quote as a form of address ("Come here, Watson.");
  2. the previous speaker in the same conversation;
  3. the same person as the speaker's previous quote;
  4. the next speaker, for an opening line.
  A conversation ends after 100 narration words without a quote; change this on the Dialogue page.
- **Speech verbs** are the verb whose subject is the mention BookNLP used to attribute the quote. Otherwise it's the nearest speech verb within 8 tokens.
- **Distinctiveness** compares an item's frequency in the target and the reference with a 2×2 table. It is ranked by your choice of log-likelihood, chi-squared, log ratio, %DIFF or odds ratio. p-values come from G² or χ², with an optional Bonferroni correction.
- **Network measures** come from networkx: betweenness and closeness use 1 ÷ weight as distance, and communities use Louvain with a fixed seed.

## Files

  - `core/`: the analysis
    - `bookdata.py` reads one book and extracts relations; `library.py` holds settings, links, tags and your corrections; `bookcollections.py` collections of books; `entitygroups.py` saved groups of entities; `plurals.py` plural groups; `view.py` combines books, thresholds and links and builds profiles
    - `stats.py` comparison statistics; `corpus.py` concordance, word lists, n-grams, collocates, keywords and reference files; `dialogue.py` quotes, speech verbs, addressees and conversations; `narrative.py` chapters and slices, arcs, style, stylometry, sentiment and lexicon arcs; `lexicons.py` your word lexicons; `narrators.py` narrator suggestions; `network.py` networks; `links.py` link suggestions; `reader.py` the text view
    - `topics/` topic modelling
  - `static/`: the browser side (`index.html`, `app.css`, and the scripts in `js/`)
- `tests/`: the tests, the generated corpus they use, and `benchmark.py`, which times every analysis over a whole library
- `alex-data/`: aLex's own files, made in the folder you start it from; it is the Default workspace, and holds `workspaces.json` and `workspaces/` for the others

The details are in [DOCUMENTATION.md](DOCUMENTATION.md); what every figure means and how it is calculated is in [MEASURES.md](MEASURES.md).

## How corrections are kept

Corrections (addressees, conversations, narrators, chapter starts and titles) are attached to quotes and paragraphs by their opening words, not by token numbers. They survive re-exports from the companion editor as long as the quote or paragraph still begins the same way. If you change a quote's opening words in the editor, correct its addressees again; a chapter start whose paragraph changed is quietly ignored.

Corrections that name an entity (a book's narrator, narrators of paragraphs and chapters, addressees, added or removed participants) follow your links: linking entities moves them to the linked person, and unlinking gives each book its own entity back, so a narrator you set never ends up pointing at a person that no longer exists. (A narrator saved before this was fixed may still name a person that is gone and show up as `p:<number>`; choose the narrator again on the **Narrators** page.)
